# Construct the Head From a Cranial Ball and Facial Wedge

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a head must be blocked from a chosen viewpoint before features are drawn
**THEN** combine a rounded cranial mass with an attached facial wedge, then change their visible dominance, overlap, and exposed planes to match that viewpoint
**ELSE** re-establish the head axis and viewing angle before placing either mass

## Do
- Set the cranial volume first, then seat the facial wedge beneath and forward of the brow region instead of drawing a flat mask on top.
- Use a vertical centerline and a curved brow or eye cross-contour to make the turn and tilt readable.
- Let the cranium dominate in a downview; let the jaw, nose, lips, brow, and other underplanes gain importance in an upview.

## Don't
- Keep the facial wedge the same apparent size and shape in every view.
- Allow the jaw block to slide away from the cranial mass or contradict its centerline.

## Checklist
- The view is identifiable without eyes, nose, mouth, hair, or ears.
- The face belongs to the cranium at one clear attachment and follows its turn.
- High and low views change the balance between cranial and facial masses rather than merely moving feature symbols.

## Notes
Hogarth treats the head as two persistent volumes whose projected relationship changes with the observer. This prevents the common flat-symbol habit in which a jaw outline is pasted onto an unchanged circle regardless of angle.
