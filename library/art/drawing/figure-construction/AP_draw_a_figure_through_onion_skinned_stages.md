# Draw a Figure Through Onion-Skinned Stages

## Objective
Carry one intended figure from a visualized pose to the requested refinement level while preserving its action, viewpoint, attachments, and depth through every successive drawing state.

## Steps / Flow
1. **Step 0 — show a rough idea of the picture.** Make a quick-and-dirty marker-like thumbnail that suggests the subject, action, camera, crop, balance, silhouette, focal priority, major prop placement, shadow or value grouping, and near-to-far arrangement. Use broad strokes and flat shapes. The thumbnail is an exploratory idea probe, not a commitment and not a registered construction layer; Stage 1 may correct pose, proportion, framing, or placement while preserving the selected concept. Do not solve anatomy, construction, features, costume, or finish here. When a pose or body part is unfamiliar enough to threaten the piece, run a brief targeted warm-up before committing to the main canvas.
2. **Step 1 — translate the idea into a simple skeleton.** Use only a sparse structural map: action line and torso centerline; head oval with one facing axis; shoulder and hip axes; single-line limbs; joint circles; simple flat symbols for rib cage and pelvis; mitten, wedge, or point markers for hands and feet; and plain lines or flat silhouettes for critical props, wings, tails, shields, weapons, and contact points. Keep the mark-making quick, dirty, and diagrammatic. Do not add limb thickness, developed three-dimensional masses, cross-contours, anatomy, facial features, hair, clothing, armor, texture, shading, environment detail, or polished silhouette. If the Stage 1 image could be mistaken for a character sketch or finished illustration when enlarged, it is overworked.
3. **Register the next sheet to the accepted skeleton.** Use the exact preceding artifact as the underdrawing, lowered-opacity layer, edit source, or light-board guide. Before using a generative image tool, verify that the runtime can target that artifact as the active image edit; if exact image-to-image control is unavailable or uncertain, stop and disclose the limit rather than generate a new interpretation and call it an overlay. Lock the canvas, camera, action, landmark positions, joint centers, member count, attachments, major proportions, and near-to-far order. Make an intentional correction in the underlying stage when one of those decisions is wrong; never restart from the verbal prompt and hope for a similar pose.
4. **Step 2 — build a plain articulated maquette and stop.** Convert the accepted framework into the simplest masses that prove the figure: cranial ball and facial wedge, rib-cage barrel, pelvic wedge, tapered limb cylinders, joint blocks, hand wedges, and foot wedges. For a human action figure, route through `AP_notate_a_figure_in_structural_order`: carry the action into the torso, then resolve legs, arms, and head in that order unless a rare support-mechanics exception is explicitly justified. Use centerlines, cross-contours, draw-through, taper, overlap, foreshortening, width, projected length, and seated attachments. A single flat separation value is allowed only when one mass cannot otherwise be read in front of another. Searching alternatives may remain faint in Step 1, but by the end of Step 2 choose one coherent position for every major mass, limb, joint, attachment, support point, and overlap; remove or clearly subordinate rejected paths. Do **not** model muscles, resolve facial identity, style hair, design fabric folds, add texture, dramatize light, polish contours, or make the block presentation-ready. Labels, arrows, panel borders, and flat symbols do not substitute for depth.
5. **Pass the Stage 2 information-budget gate and lock the block.** Ignore explanatory text and read only the plain construction: identify which masses face the viewer, which recede, what overlaps, where every limb attaches, and how the figure carries or supports its weight. Then remove any Stage 3 or Stage 4 information that slipped in. If anatomy, facial detail, costume detail, line polish, or lighting is doing work needed to understand the pose, the block has failed and must be simplified until the structure carries that information itself. Treat the accepted block as the principal controlling reference for Steps 3 and 4. Do not advance while competing limb positions, attachments, contact points, or major overlaps remain unresolved, or when Step 3 would still have to decide the pose instead of developing it. Repair a failed region by briefly restating its local framework and rebuilding that block while preserving unaffected content; return to the complete framework when the local repair cannot integrate.
6. **Step 3 — describe the specific figure on a new registered layer.** Turn the generic masses into the intended anatomy, character identity, expression, hair masses, costume, creature structure, or designed body; connect forms through believable transitions and select increasingly specific contours. Controlled form modeling may clarify the organic structure, but the accepted Stage 2 masses, attachments, and depth order remain fixed. Stop before final spectacle: complete lighting, material effects, atmospheric integration, texture finish, and presentation polish belong to Stage 4.
7. **Step 4 — present the resolved figure.** Reject weak or contradictory lines, repair any remaining structural fault through the underlying construction, and finish with the chosen line, value, light, color, edge, material, texture, atmosphere, and polish treatment. Use rendering to clarify volume and priority rather than to conceal unresolved anatomy or attachment.
8. **Deliver only the requested state, then read backward.** Hidden stages still govern a final-only request; visible stage sequences remain registered to the same artifact. After every generative edit, compare camera, crop, silhouette, joint centers, attachments, major masses, depth order, and unaffected objects with the controlling image. Reject a drifting result instead of relabeling it as the requested stage. Reduce the accepted result from final to rough, block, framework, and original intent. When the pose, composition, member count, or attachments change during that reduction, return to the earliest stage where the drift entered.

## Notes
This is the drawing-medium specialization of the universal thumbnail-to-final workflow. Its central production constraint is onion skinning: each stage is a developed state of one drawing, not an independent interpretation of the same prompt. A later pass may correct an earlier decision, but it should do so deliberately and carry that correction through the underlying construction rather than silently redesigning the figure. The stage boundary can be remembered as: **Stage 0 shows a rough idea; Stage 1 locates everything; Stage 2 solves the body; Stage 3 describes the body; Stage 4 presents the body.** A beautiful Stage 1 or Stage 2 that already describes or presents the figure is overworked, not advanced.

The figure-mass pattern supplies the general Step 2 move: preserve the action while placing connected masses. For human action figures, the structural-order AP supplies the subordinate sequence and routes local torso, leg, arm, foot, and head decisions to their Chapter 2 patterns. The Chapter 4 size-control AP strengthens the block when foreshortened members make length unreliable. Other construction, anatomy, lighting, or rendering patterns can be chained into the stage where their decision is needed as the library grows. A Drill may be run before the piece as a warm-up or during a failed stage as a low-cost side study; the result of the Drill informs the main artifact but does not replace it.

Skill retrieval and image registration are separate success criteria. First verify that SkillForge was actually active and that the relevant procedure entered context. Then verify onion-skinned continuity directly: the exact prior image must be used as the edit target and the result must pass a registration check. Do not infer either condition from repository files merely being present.

A drawing must make forms exist in space. A clean diagram can explain names or relationships, but it does not satisfy this procedure when the requested product is a sketch, figure study, illustration, or render. Depth must survive without captions through orientation, overlap, recession, attachment, support, and turning planes.
