# Seat the Neck Inside the Upper Chest Opening

## Pattern Rule
**IF** a neck and head are being attached to a blocked torso
**THEN** keep the lower neck seated inside the fixed upper chest opening and continue its center through the torso or implied spine while the upper neck and head turn above that root
**ELSE** correct the torso opening before testing another head direction

## Do
- Draw the socket or neck-pit region before changing head tilt so every trial uses the same lower attachment.
- Let chest or upper back overlap the neck when the view demands it; hidden attachment is not relocated attachment.
- Check neck length only after the root, axis, and head support are structurally correct.

## Don't
- Grow the neck from a shoulder joint, chest edge, or a new opening created for each head trial.
- Keep the head direction while allowing the cervical column to miss the torso center entirely.

## Checklist
- The lower neck remains inside one stable opening for every head variation.
- The neck axis can be continued into the torso without a sideways jump.
- Any overlap hides the root without implying decapitation or a second socket.

## Notes
The chapter describes the neck as a short column emerging from within the triangulated hollow of the chest. Guided review made the fixed-root consequence explicit: head freedom occurs above the attachment, not by sliding the base across the shoulder girdle.
