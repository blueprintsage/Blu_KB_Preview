# Join Rib Cage and Pelvis Through a Flexible Waist

## Pattern Rule
**IF** the upper and lower torso must bend, tip, or oppose one another
**THEN** block the rib cage as a barrel and the pelvis as a wedge, orient each independently, and join them through a broad flexible waist carrying one continuous centerline
**ELSE** reduce the pose to two connected masses before attaching limbs

## Do
- Use the erect counterpoise as a baseline: chest barrel tending back, pelvic wedge tending forward.
- Let the waist compress on one side and stretch on the other while remaining a continuous volume.
- Use the combined kidney-like silhouette only as a summary of the joined masses, not as a replacement for their separate orientations.

## Don't
- Connect the two masses with a thin post, stack of rings, hose, or exposed spine.
- Let the centerline stop at each mass so the chest and pelvis have no readable relationship.

## Checklist
- The barrel and wedge can face differently without becoming separate objects.
- One centerline travels through both masses and explains the transition.
- The side contours show a believable stretch/compression pair instead of equal empty gaps.

## Notes
The chapter presents the waist as the flexible axial region that locks the body's two largest masses together. This is the structural basis for later turns: independent movement is permitted, disconnection is not.
